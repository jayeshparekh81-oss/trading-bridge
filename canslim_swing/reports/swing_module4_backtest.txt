9. WHAT THE MACHINE ACTUALLY DID (description, not verdict)

  Over 987 sessions the engine turned 3,973 candidate-day signals into
  160 fills — roughly 3.4 entries per trading month. The
  dominant reason a signal never became a trade was the BREAKOUT ITSELF: 3,205
  signals (81%) never traded through pivot x1.001, against 429
  (11%) that would have filled but met a full 6-slot book and 177
  already held in that symbol. The chase-cap almost never bound (2 cancellations),
  so gap-aways were not a practical constraint at these settings.

  NOTE ON THESE COUNTERS: the price test is evaluated BEFORE the portfolio
  constraints, so `skip_no_slot` contains only orders that genuinely would
  have filled. An earlier build bucketed slot-blocked orders first, which
  inflated that bucket to 1,958 and made the 6-slot cap look like the binding
  constraint; it is not. The fill SET is identical under either ordering —
  only the accounting differed — but the conclusion drawn from it inverted.

  Capital sat about 49% deployed on average, with all six slots
  occupied on 383 of 987 sessions and the book completely empty on
  175. Because the market filter gates entries but never forces an exit,
  positions opened before a nifty_off stretch simply ran on through it; the
  book drains during those stretches only as individual exits fire, which is
  why exposure decays rather than steps down when the market filter flips.

  Where the money moved, by exit reason: sma50_break -258,491 Rs (92 trades), stop -500,394 Rs (41 trades), target +925,309 Rs (27 trades).
  The trend-break exit did most of the work by count (92 of 160 exits)
  while the 25% target fired 27 times and the 8% stop 41 times. Win rate
  was 31.9% against an average win of 22,219 Rs and an average loss of
  -8,869 Rs, i.e. the arithmetic depends on the win/loss SIZE ratio rather
  than on hit rate — the loss-cutting rules, not the entry, set the shape of
  the R distribution.

  The stop is live from the fill day onward, as specified: 3 trades were
  stopped or targeted on their own entry day. That check is worth 59,252 Rs of
  reported profit — an earlier build ran the day's exit sweep before entries
  existed, so fill-day stops were silently skipped and net PnL read 225,675 Rs
  instead of the figure above. It was caught by adversarial review, not by the
  self-audit that build printed, which counted same-day round-trips with a
  statistic that was zero by construction under the very ordering it excused.

  These are descriptions of one frozen parameter set on one universe. Nothing
  here has been swept, and no significance is claimed or implied.
